My father in law and I sat in section 110 for the first Kingfish game Friday night, and I enjoyed the heck out of it.

The food was better. The hockey was solid. Baton Rouge beat Monroe 6 to 2. There are worse ways to spend an evening.

After an offseason of roster questions, predictions and visa concerns, it was nice to sit down and watch some actual hockey. And by the end of the weekend, the Kingfish had given us plenty to feel good about.

Friday’s score wasn’t just the result of a few lucky bounces. Baton Rouge held a 41 to 11 advantage in shots through two periods. The lead was only 2 to 1 at that point, but the Kingfish were making Monroe work.

Then the power play got busy.

Three goals during the major penalty early in the third period opened things up. Jake Cox finished with two goals, Joseph Drapluk contributed, and Matt Stoia made an immediate impression. We talked before the season about Cox having a chance to benefit from the players around him. That was a pretty encouraging first look.

I also want to give the food improvement its due. This is Baton Rouge. What you’re eating while you watch matters. A good game helps, obviously, but the whole evening is what makes somebody want to buy another ticket. From my seat, opening night gave me plenty of reasons to come back.

Then the Kingfish went to Monroe on Saturday and won by the same score.

That one required a different kind of effort. Baton Rouge trailed after the first period, tied it in the second, and scored four times in the third. Jason Brancheau scored twice, and Scott Shorrock somehow found time to score while the Kingfish were down two skaters.

Scoring while killing a five on three is a fairly impolite thing to do in somebody else’s building. I approve.

But Ed Coffey deserves a big part of this conversation. He stopped 35 of 37 shots Saturday and gave the Kingfish a chance to take control later. We spent plenty of time questioning the goaltending before the season. Coffey earned some credit this weekend.

Two games won’t settle every question. There will be tougher nights, and we still have plenty to learn about this team. But winning at home and then following it up on the road is a good start for Jimmy Soper’s group. So is getting contributions from several different players.

I thought the entire first weekend was a success. The home opener was an enjoyable night out with my father in law, the team played well, and the road win gave us something else to smile about Saturday.

Our prediction was 30 wins.

Two down. I’m enjoying this so far.
