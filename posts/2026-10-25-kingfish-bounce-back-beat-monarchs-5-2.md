The Baton Rouge Kingfish needed a response Sunday afternoon. Back on home ice, they delivered one.

After dropping consecutive games in Southaven, the Kingfish defeated the Mid-South Monarchs 5-2 at Raising Cane's River Center, salvaging the final game of their three-game weekend series and improving to 3-2 on the young FPHL season.

It was a welcome change from the frustration of Friday and Saturday, when Baton Rouge generated plenty of offensive opportunities but couldn't find enough goals to overcome the Monarchs.

Sunday's five-goal performance matched the Kingfish's combined offensive output from the previous two nights.

## A Weekend of Missed Opportunities—and a Strong Finish

Mid-South entered Sunday's contest riding three consecutive victories, including a 4-3 win Friday and a 3-2 victory Saturday.

Saturday's defeat was particularly frustrating. Baton Rouge put 43 shots on goal but managed only two against Tommy Nappier, who turned away 41 shots. The Kingfish also accumulated 51 penalty minutes and surrendered two power-play goals.

Those difficulties made Sunday's response especially important. Rather than allowing the weekend to become a three-game sweep, Baton Rouge finished with a convincing three-goal victory.

The result leaves the Kingfish at 3-2 and Mid-South at 3-4. Both teams have three victories, but Baton Rouge has played two fewer games, giving the Fish an early advantage in winning percentage.

There is still plenty of hockey ahead, but divisional matchups carry additional importance in a league where familiar opponents meet repeatedly throughout the season.

## Questions Remain

The victory provides some breathing room, but several developments deserve attention.

Goaltender Anthony Seykora left Saturday's contest with an injury, forcing Ed Coffey into relief duty. Seykora's status remains unconfirmed, and Sunday's complete goaltending statistics were not yet available.

The Kingfish also face questions about consistency. They've scored five or more goals in all three victories this season but managed only three and two goals in their two losses.

That's a pretty noticeable difference, and something worth watching as the season progresses.

Still, there's something to be said for a team that can bounce back after two frustrating losses and take care of business at home.

## A Better Experience Inside the Tank

Beyond the action on the ice, there are some encouraging signs that the Kingfish are continuing to improve the overall fan experience at the Raising Cane's River Center.

The PA sound was noticeably better Sunday afternoon, and Mike continues to improve with the in-game entertainment. The addition of new games and activities during breaks is a welcome touch, giving fans something to enjoy between periods and during stoppages.

And in a personal highlight, I even managed to catch a T-shirt floating down on a parachute. That's a first for me.

The Sunday afternoon crowd was smaller and quieter than opening weekend, although it still felt like a decent turnout. The energy wasn't quite the same, but that's not entirely surprising for an afternoon game.

The encouraging part is that the organization appears to be making adjustments and finding ways to improve the experience. Those details matter, particularly for a young franchise trying to build a loyal following.

Winning hockey games will bring people through the doors, but creating an entertaining afternoon or evening at the Tank is what will keep them coming back.

## What's Next?

Baton Rouge heads west next weekend for its first meetings with the expansion Stockton Thunder.

The teams play Friday, October 30, and Saturday, October 31, at Adventist Health Arena in Stockton, California.

Sunday's victory ensures they'll make that trip with some momentum.

The Kingfish aren't going to win every game, and there will undoubtedly be some growing pains along the way. But five games into the season, there's plenty to like about this team and the direction the organization is heading.

And after Sunday's performance, it's nice to have a win to talk about again.
