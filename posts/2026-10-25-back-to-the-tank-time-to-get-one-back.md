I’m looking forward to being back in the Tank today. Opening night with my father in law in section 110 left a pretty good impression: better food, solid hockey, and a team that gave us plenty to cheer about. Now we get a new opponent in the building.

Unfortunately, the Mid-South Monarchs are arriving with two wins over us already packed in their bags. I’d prefer they leave the third one here.

Friday’s 4–3 loss was frustrating because the Kingfish had opportunities to take control. Joe Drapluk and Dmitry Kuznetsov built a 2–0 lead, and Matt Stoia’s shorthanded goal put Baton Rouge ahead again heading into the third. Then the lead disappeared just 15 seconds into the period. Mid-South added another, and the Fish couldn’t find an answer despite 17 shots in the final frame. Ed Coffey finished with 24 saves.

Saturday brought another close loss, this time 3–2, and a different kind of frustration. Tyler Williams opened the scoring, Scott Shorrock gave the Fish a late chance, and Baton Rouge still came home empty handed. Giving Mid-South nine power plays certainly didn’t help. The Monarchs scored on two of them, while the Kingfish went 0-for-5.

You can argue about individual calls. You still have to find a way to stop spending so much of the game killing penalties.

There’s also something to watch in goal. Tony Seykora’s first professional start ended early with an injury Saturday, bringing Coffey into the game for the final 23 minutes. Seykora had stopped all nine shots he faced in the opening period. Until there’s an update, I’m not going to guess about his availability.

Today, I want to see the Kingfish make life harder around the opposing crease. Thomas Nappier has won both games for Mid-South, and another afternoon of letting a comfortable goalie see everything coming probably won’t get it done. Traffic, rebounds, and somebody willing to stay in the unpleasant part of the ice would be welcome.

Williams is a bright spot, with a point in all four games. But the bigger question is whether Baton Rouge can finish. The third periods that looked so good against Monroe have gone the other direction this weekend.

I’m still encouraged. Two losses by one goal don’t erase opening weekend. They do show how quickly a turnover or a trip to the penalty box can change things.

My prediction? The Fish get one back in a close game, provided they spend more time playing hockey than watching it from the box.

A new opponent, another afternoon in the Tank, and a chance to send everybody home feeling better about the weekend. I’m ready.
